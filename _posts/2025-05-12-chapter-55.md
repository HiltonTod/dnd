After the companions discuss The Lady's visit and the limited information she revealed, it's late morning
and they're all feeling a bit restless. The cool, outside air should help. Exiting Lord Neverwinter's manor,
aptly named Castle Never, they head south towards the Neverdeath Graveyard.

They cross the river that runs westward, towards the bay, and divides the city's northern and southern sides.
It's not long before they find themselves meandering the stone streets of Protector's Enclave, a thriving market
district. As The Lady said, most things look familiar. Most of the town folk wore practical clothing, homespun
from rough wool and linen. The city's guard dressed in leather armor, cheap enough to buy in bulk while strong
enough to afford the level protection usually needed in a well-behaved metropolis. Few shopkeepers hawked their
wares verbally in the streets, most relying on their reputations and fancy storefront displays to draw in customers.

Bilwin stops for a moment, perusing the beer offerings of a street vendor. After purchasing a pint he ponders aloud,
"I want a battle hammer, or maybe an axe. I've been using my [hurdy-gurdy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hurdy-gurdy),
while it's safely in its case, mind you, but it's not quite as effective as a good old fashioned hammer that's
designed to turn an enemy's head inside out."

"It's been a long while since I was able to enter a proper magic shop, like that one." Mond points further down
the road, where a sign hangs off the front of a brick building. The metal work portrays a sigil, inlaid with leafed
gold and carved deep into a piece of what appears to be ironwood. Concentric rings and intertwined runes pulse with
a low, warm light around the name, The Gilded Glyph. 

Looking back to the dwarf, "They might have a special hammer or axe that you take a liking to."

Nextdoor to The Gilded Glyph, is a jeweler shop and the entrance is two steps down, below street level. What catches
their eye at first is that the door is only four and a half feet tall. Carved in the stone above the door, the store's
name is written in dwarven. Gven reads it and a short burst of laughter escapes between her tusks. The others look
quizzically at her and then to Bilwin.

The dwarf grins and points at the words inscribed above the door. "That translates to 'Fucking Duck' and the words
carved into the door say," he points a little lower at more dwarven characters. "Screw you, you can duck."

---

Mond is the first to pass through The Gilded Glyph's door and a light, airy melody fills the shop—it's neither too
loud nor too quiet, it's the perfect volume. At first glance, the inside is much larger than it should be. Carefully
studying the layout, Mond realizes there's magic at play with the architectural design. The smell of incense permeates
the too-large room; it's a perfectly balanced blend of sandalwood and sage—once again, neither too strong nor too weak,
the perfect smell. Old parchments are neatly rolled up and stored in oak cabinets with glass
fronts that open upward, preventing the dust from ruining their longevity. Along one side, an organized flurry of
potions and scrolls are floating in the air, rearranging and inserting themselves into different spots on the
wooden shelves and glass-front cabinets.

Bilwin ambles into the shop behind Dolor and immediately takes in a deep breath, "Ahhh, that's a wonderful smell of
freshly quarried limestone with a hint of lichen! Where is that coming from? Oh my goodness, are those vials floating?
Look, they're putting themselves to bed. How do they do that?"

On the other side of the store, much further than physics should allow it to be, given the front door's proximity
to Fucking Duck, stands a half-elven woman. Her silky black hair falls in straight strands to her waist, filled with
streaks of silver and parted across the center of her head. Deep azure robes appear to glide along her shoulders,
then drape around her arms and reach the floor—hidden behind the counter at which she stands.

"Wipe your feet."

After dutifully cleaning their footwear on the fluffy welcome mat inside the door, the group meanders over to the
counter along separate pathways through the enticing merchandise. 

"I am Maelis Varn, owner of The Gilded Glyph and scholar of innate esoteric arcane manipulations!"

Unsurprisingly, Bilwin is the first respond. "Oh, that sounds lovely! And important, too. But also lovely.
I'm looking for a magic battle axe or hammer. You know, something that works better in battle than my hurdy-gurdy.
Which I keep in its case when I'm smacking bad folk upside the head. But an axe would be better, don't you think?"

Without any expression on her face, she looks over the dwarf for a solid minute, sizing him up, and then recedes
through a door behind the counter—presumably to a storeroom. An odd quiet emanates from within the room, except for
a single utterance. "Seriously, that's where you went off to sulk. If sentience is going to make you so emotional
and sullen, I'll enchant another poleaxe. No warrior wants a mighty weapon that is always off pouting."

Maelis returns to the counter holding a battle axe. It's not pretty, by any means or from any perspective, but it
appears to be solid and exactly the proper length for a dwarf of Bilwin's size. "This is the only one I have at
the moment, but it's a good, sturdy axe that'll bash in brains better and for longer than most. The enchantment
gives you a boost with your attacks and the damage done."

The cleric reaches over the counter to take the battle axe and feel its weight. One or two swings—carefully
orchestrated, mind you, to not break any other wares—reveal that it's perfectly sized and weighted. "This is
it, thanks!"

"Anything else for you fine folks?"

---

Grindlefoot is the last of the companions to enter the street. The purple-hued oak door to The Gilded Glyph swings
closed silently and effortlessly, because of its magically enhanced movement. Maelis' soft voice reaches them easily
from inside, "Lovely doing business with you! Come back soon, and best of luck in the graveyard."

The halfling is wearing a new item around his neck, the Collar of Breath and Blood. A deep red crystal is set into
the plain, sturdy bronze collet—Dolor described that as the cup that encloses the gem—and secured with a filigreed
bezel around its outer edges. The Collar enhances Grindlefoot's constitution, allows him to cast
[Enhance Ability - Bear's Endurance](https://www.dndbeyond.com/spells/2083-enhance-ability) once per long rest, and
he gains advantage when concentrating on his spells. Although his most noticeable, that wasn't his only purchase from
the arcane scholar. His favorite discovery was a Seed Pouch of Old Barrows, with magical seeds that yield healing herbs
and a slightly strange hybrid fruit that can have random effects on whomever eats one. The bag refills with every cycle
of the new moon.
