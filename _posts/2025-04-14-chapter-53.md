Holding the trident named Wave, Dolor realizes that they've solved the riddle. The tiefling looks around at
his companions. Bilwin stand's tall after the battle, Whelm clutched tightly in his hand and his head tipped
a little to the side, as though he's listening to something. Next to him, Gven still kneels in the sand with
her hand resting on Tempest Edge's pommel, the greatsword saving her from falling to the ground in exhaustion.
Grindlefoot stands off to the side, slightly distant from the others, his bowler hat askew but unharmed. As
usual, Mond remained out of melee range and is now peering into the boiling water behind the translucent barrier,
trying to understand how it works.

The companions are tired from combat and uncertain about what to do next, when Gustaf comes running into
their midst. "Get it off!" He's clawing at his back, desperately trying to remove Blackrazor. After several
frantic moments, the blade slips free and clatters to the ground.

Whelm, the magical mace in Bilwin's hand, begins to vibrate. Subtle at first, and then undeniable. Soon it's
unbearable. Dwarven hands are renowned for their strength and the cleric's face reveals his effort to hold on to
the mace. The shaking continues to intensify, becoming too much for even his grip, and his fingers finally release
it. 

Dolor's hand starts to grow cold, the trident he only just discovered and claimed begins to show signs of frost
along its length. The razor sharp tips glow an iridescent blue as they reach temperatures as cold as the darkest
depths of the sea. The cold burns, it's too much, the tiefling's fingers release on instinct.

All three weapons hit the floor. A deep silence follows.

They begin to glow. A hum rises from them, eerie and harmonic. A resonance not of sound, but of pressure—felt in
their bones.

Then a _thrum_ fills the vast cavern. Deep. Not heard, but _felt_. 

Something shifts around them and each of them gasps—their lungs full of air and yet starved of oxygen. The ground
beneath their feet feels oppressively solid, and terrifyingly insubstantial. They're rooted in place and adrift
all at once.

Above them, the space between worlds tears open, thin as a whisper. A rift. Beyond, inside the sliver of an opening,
something watches. Waits. Patiently.

"Och, nay...nay! Had I kent, I wid ne'er hae—"

Thamnoki stumbles forward, eyes wide in horror. He grabs the trident, Wave, from the ground at Dolor's feet and
with a furious cry, hurls it like a bolt of lightning into the air.

The trident pierces the membrane. It stretches—groans—and then rips wide open. The boiling sea rushes in.

A wall of scalding water crashes into the chamber. The companions are slammed backward, their lungs punched
empty of air. The force is crushing. The heat is unbearable. Attempts to scream only let the water fill their
mouths. They reach for each other, for anything—but the flood surges faster than they can react.

The water isn't just drowning them, it's boiling them alive. Every breath is pain. Their limbs thrash every
which way. The chamber collapses around them, darkness swirls in, endless and cold and burning.
