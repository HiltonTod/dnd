Bilwin is still at the harp, working through the second line of "Mary Had a Little Lamb" off the dead priest's sheet music, when the door opens. Gven's arms still feel hollow where the shadow drank from them, and none of the five has had a minute's rest since the library. A fourth robed figure leans in, a little out of breath, as anyone would be who ran up two flights of stairs late for a recital. "Oh, man. Sorry to miss the opening song. I finally made it."

Taking in the room, the priest finds the row of chairs, three bodies on the carpet, one of them without its head, and five strangers looking back. Their face falls. "Oh, fuck."

Reaching into their robe before Gven can invite them in, the priest tosses a small blue crystal onto the carpet at the companions' feet. It bursts in a cloud of glittering, twirling dust, and the last thing any of them hears in the conservatory is the priest's voice, already a long way off: "Well, fuck this recital."

Then there's nothing at all, and in the dark Landro speaks into all five of their heads. "I would like it noted that I have done this before, and I did not enjoy it then either."

---

The light changes first. A moment ago it was candles in a conservatory, and now it's sunlight, clean and bright, the first real sun any of them have seen since Neverwinter, pouring through a broad viewport of shimmering crystal. Beyond it there's no gray mist and no ash, only green fields, a river catching the light, and far off on the horizon a column of smoke where something is burning. Far below, scaffolding climbs the legs of a colossus, and work crews move around its feet like ants.

They're standing in a steel chamber thirty feet high, every rivet new and every plate polished. On a raised platform a captain's chair of wood and steel faces the viewport, its right armrest ending in a small, empty, cup-shaped cradle, and a round silver helmet sits on the platform beside it. Asleep in the chair, mouth open and snoring gently, is a man in Cyran regalia.

Dolor knows the chair.

The last time he stood in this room a skeleton was slumped in it, with a dent in its helmet and a gemstone in the bones of its right hand. He looks at the man for a long moment and says nothing. Stamped above the helmet's brow is a small insignia, the mark of his parents' workshop, the same one he found on it in this room before. Watching the man in the chair, Dolor doesn't notice it.

"I knew him," says Landro. "He was not good at his work. He was, however, present."

Waking with a snort to find five strangers on his platform, the pilot complains before he says anything useful. "Those damn engineers. Always sending people up here to tell me what to do. Look, I'm the pilot, I drive this thing, and it's the biggest colossus anyone has ever built. But the control crystal is missing, and I can't pilot without it. So that's your job. I know you're mercenaries, but you came highly regarded, and this fellow is going along to make sure you do it right."

The fellow isn't someone anyone would expect. The first thing the companions notice is a great domed shell with moss growing over one side of it, and under it an old [tortle](https://dnd5e.wikidot.com/lineage:tortle), hunched and walking on two legs, his beak chipped at the tip, leaning on what looks like a cane until it turns out to be a battle axe. "Call me Shellbert. Are we doing some work, or what?"

The pilot carries on as though there's been no interruption. "The crystal's west of here, with an old woman who lives out there alone. Granny Verdigris, that's what they call her. Fly over, have some tea, and come back with it. There's a transport docked at the mouth. I'd go myself, but I can't leave the chair. The chair is too important."

Bilwin wants to know whether they can bring the old woman back too, and the pilot would rather they didn't, because the engineers don't like dealing with the elderly—unless they're turtles. Grindlefoot suggests bringing the chair instead. It's attached.

Dolor hasn't taken his eyes off the pilot's seat. "Don't get too used to the chair."

On the way out, Bilwin says what the rest of them are working around. "Did we just travel back in time?"

"That is my assumption," says Landro.

---

The spinal shaft is a ten-foot cylinder of faint green light a few steps from the pilot's door, and the last time the companions saw it, it was choked with rubble from the chest up. Not waiting for anyone, Shellbert tips backwards off the floor like a diver going over the side of a boat and drifts gently upwards, axe and all.

Twenty feet above, the shaft opens onto a chamber with a massive stone table covered in maps and scroll cases. Stepping off into the room, Bilwin walks straight to the table and throws both arms around it. "It's so nice to see you again! I see you've got your maps back. That's so great." The table shifts very slightly under the hug and then holds still. Shellbert looks at the dwarf embracing the furniture and decides he doesn't want to know.

Another twenty feet up, a tangle of humming metal hangs down into the shaft like the uvula at the back of a throat. Every one of the companions remembers the last time someone touched it, and they all tell the tortle so at once. His hand stops an inch short.

Above the throat is the mouth, open to the sky at the far end, a three-foot ledge and then nothing but wind. A magical cannon the length of a longboat sits on mountings that Dolor once found torn out from the inside, crewed now and pointed out at the green land. Docked on the ledge is the transport, a long covered skiff of wood and steel, jointed and ribbed like something halfway to alive, with no one at its helm. Raising a hand to the gun crew on his way past, Shellbert gives them his blessing. "Men! Good work. We're off to see the granny!"

Climbing aboard last, Grindlefoot spends the first minute looking at each of the others in turn, deeply suspicious, as though someone was told not to push the button and the button has been pushed anyway. No one has. The skiff simply waits until the last of them is inside, as if it has been counting, and lifts off on its own.

---

Cyre goes by underneath them green and alive. Far to the south, colossi slug it out on a sunlit plain with warforged pouring between their feet, the same war Dolor watched in the vision Alamar-Vatashi gave him. After half an hour the skiff passes high over a village of proper streets busy with carts, a windmill turning its wood-and-canvas sails beside a sturdy mill building, and a statue at the central crossroads, clean and bare.

Pressing his face to the side of the skiff, Bilwin squints down at it. "I swear we've been here before."

"That is a village," Landro observes. "There are people in it. I did not know that there were people in it."

"Neither did I." Dolor watches the crossroads slide past. The last time he walked those streets they were mud, every face in them belonged to a warforged, and the statue was wrapped in colored cloths to mourn the people walking past it now. "That's Ialos. That's where we met Mercy."

The river below runs wide and clear, nothing like the two feet of dirty water the companions once drifted north above as clouds. No one says much after that.

Half an hour further west, the skiff sets down in front of a small cottage at the foot of the mountains, far from any road, with a neat garden and a great many wind chimes. It's exactly the kind of house a grandmother lives in.

Bilwin considers it. "I was expecting a shoe."

---

Taking the path, Dolor reaches the door with Shellbert hobbling behind him, keeping up without any visible difficulty. The door creaks open a hand's width on a very old human woman, shorter than Dolor and taller than Grindlefoot, who looks up at the six of them as though they've tracked something in. When Grindlefoot explains that they're after a crystal for piloting the big colossuses, she brightens. "Colossi. Ten points to the little one, for not knowing how to say colossi. Come in, then."

The cottage is larger on the inside than the outside, and the swordfish mounted opposite the door is one Mond is almost certain wasn't there until he thought of one. In every other way it's a small house, but the chairs are all different heights, the table legs are different lengths, none of the pots match, and everything is slightly wrong in a way that seems arranged to start an argument.

"Crystals," she says, hobbling past a squat round table sitting next to a chair alongside the fireplace. "There's a bowl of them there. Take one. Whichever you want."

Turning the signet ring once on his finger, Dolor speaks cautiously, slowly, as though he's already questioning the old woman's wits. "The one we need controls a construct east of here the size of a mountain, and it can't move without the piece you have. We were told you had a crystal that can hold that kind of power, and we were sent to procure it from you."

"Six points to the tiefling, he knows what he's talking about, and two more for using the word procure. Minus one to the turtle." She squints round at Shellbert. "I don't know what you're doing."

"I'm listening."

Bilwin earns three points for wandering the cottage picking things up and putting them back. Shellbert says he doesn't like the way this scoring works, and loses ten for standing on her favorite rug. He claims five back, since she never told him it was her favorite. It's embroidered on it, she points out, in a careful border of green thread, if he can read. The tortle looks down, allows that it's a fair point, and steps off it.

Five more bowls of crystals have appeared on the table, heaped full. "Take one. Take two. Take a whole bowl. You're all still behind the sorcerer in points, by the way, and he's barely said a word." Shellbert takes a piece of chalk out of his pack, reaches round behind himself, and starts keeping the tally on his own shell.

From the top of Mond's staff, Landro observes into all five of their heads, "I have not been awarded any points. I would like it noted that I have not been trying."

---

The crystal at the head of Mond's staff begins to shake, only a little, a hum in the wood under his hand. Asking whether that's her doing, the sorcerer makes the mistake of calling her a witch.

"Who are you calling a witch? What do I look like?" She holds up a hand before he can answer. "Don't. Minus ten."

Getting nowhere with her, he asks the crystal instead, inside his own head. "I feel it," Landro tells him. "It is close."

Carrying the staff in a slow circle around the room, Mond feels the hum strengthen here and there but never enough to say where. "It's in this room. That's all I can tell you." Meanwhile, Grindlefoot licks one of the crystals and puts it back, receiving ten points from the elderly woman.

Deciding the simplest answer is to take her along after all, Bilwin announces that he's going to pick her up and carry her back to the pilot. Before he's taken a step, a translucent sphere shimmers up around him and seals him in. The dwarf considers it for a moment, then walks straight through it, gets both arms around the old woman's middle, and lifts her off the floor.

"Put me down this instant! Minus ten. And I was going to make you all tea."

"Do you have any beer?"

"No, but I'd bet you do." He admits that he does, sets her down, and allows that tea and beer are both brewed, so he'll try the tea. He asks, hopefully, whether it's kombucha. "Never touch the stuff." The teapot lifts itself off the stove and pours.

Leaning towards the others, the dwarf drops his voice to what he believes is a whisper. "Let's find the crystal and not tell her."

"I can hear you."

---

Watching her rather than the room, Dolor puts it together. "No one told you we were coming."

"No one told me. I've been expecting you all the same, because someone always comes, and someone always comes because I want them to." She settles into the chair by the fire. "Before anyone asks, I don't need anything from you. Entertainment is what I seek."

Shellbert looks up from the tally on his shell. "We're here to entertain you?"

"Twenty points to the turtle!" Reaching round behind himself with the chalk, Shellbert marks it on his shell.

"Fortunately," says Bilwin, "we brought a bard." A gleaming piano appears in front of him, and every one of the companions says "Don't" at the same moment. He looks at it with real longing. "I'd play you the hurdy-gurdy, but I left it at home."

Glancing at the crystal humming on Mond's staff, the dwarf has a sudden thought. "If we've gone back in time, that's the one we're looking for. It's the same crystal." He reaches for it with [Locate Object](https://www.dndbeyond.com/spells/2170-locate-object).

"Oh, let me save you the trouble," says Granny Verdigris, and every crystal in all six bowls becomes a perfect copy of the one on the staff.

Dolor studies her. "You're not on our side, are you?"

"Are there sides? I'm getting less entertained by the minute."

The location spell pulls Bilwin two ways, one towards Mond's staff and the other towards the middle of the room, somewhere about her chair. Watching her face while the dwarf turns in slow circles, Shellbert reads one old recluse in another. "She's playing with us. We're the entertainment."

Turning the ring twice, Dolor tries once more, keeping his voice as even and reasonable as he can make it. "It would mean a great deal if you'd help us. More people are depending on that crystal than you know." It's the best argument he's made all day, and she likes it.

"That's the deal, then. But you'll have to fight me for it."

She gets up out of the chair, all four and a half feet of her, and puts up her fists.

<!-- Round 1 -->

With Mond holding a spell for the moment she attacks, she goes for Dolor first, without moving from where she stands. A [shard of psychic force](http://dnd5e.wikidot.com/spell:mind-spike) simply arrives inside the tiefling's head. "What do you say to that, sonny?"

The sorcerer's [domination](https://www.dndbeyond.com/spells/2619148-dominate-person) goes off on cue, cast higher than he has ever needed, and she laughs at it. "Minus ten."

Reaching out to tap her on the shoulder with a spell crackling at his fingertips, Dolor finds she has leaned aside. "Don't dirty my dress."

A [thick vine](http://dnd5e.wikidot.com/spell:grasping-vine) bursts out of the ground beyond the door at Grindlefoot's word and lashes in to yank her outside, and she isn't where it arrives. "Don't tickle me."

Planting his feet on the favorite rug, Shellbert squares up to her. "Might be your favorite carpet, but it's my lawn. Get off my lawn!" His [rage](https://www.thegamer.com/dungeons-dragons-dnd-barbarian-rage-explained-guide/) comes up with a crack and [wild magic](http://dnd5e.wikidot.com/barbarian:wild-magic) with it, a beam of light shooting out of his underside straight at her. The old woman takes it full in the face and shrugs it off. Rubbing his belly, the tortle winces. "Heartburn." Then the battle axe comes down twice and both strikes land, and she looks at her arm where he hit her. "That smarts."

Aiming past Shellbert's shell, Bilwin sends a [bolt of light](https://www.dndbeyond.com/spells/2619136-guiding-bolt) wide. Mond's [Fire Bolt](https://www.dndbeyond.com/spells/2618890-fire-bolt) goes wider and sets the kitchen cabinets alight; she waves a hand over her shoulder without looking, and the fire goes out.

Having waited to find out whether this is really a fight, Gven calls up her rage, which she finds a little embarrassing to spend on a woman this size. The barbarian shrugs, "This is a little old lady, but okay." Tempest Edge comes up out of the longcoat, the first swing misses, and the second opens a long cut across the old woman's side.

"That didn't feel good."

Then she turns on Shellbert, and the psychic shard goes through the tortle like a nail, and the light in his belly goes out as something else wakes up in its place.

Measuring the room, Dolor sends a [wave of thunderous force](https://www.dndbeyond.com/spells/2619184-thunderwave) rolling out at her. It carries her ten feet across the cottage and bounces her off the wall beside the scorched cabinets, and she straightens her dress, delighted. "Now that was a neat little trick. Twenty points!"

Grindlefoot's vine comes in from the doorway a second time and misses a second time, so the halfling shrugs and turns into a [wolf](https://www.dndbeyond.com/monsters/17062-wolf).

"Fifteen points to the little one for trying so hard," she announces, and frowns at the wolf. "Wait. Where did the little one go?"

Backing up to give himself room, Shellbert throws his battle axe. It glows with force as it leaves his hand, catches the old woman across the shoulder, and flies straight back to him, and he throws it again. "You've got some spunk in you, turtle."

"Can we stop fighting?" Bilwin asks. "You can keep the turtle. We take the crystal and go."

"Minus two. This is fun."

Without so much as looking round, she sets laughter going in the middle of Mond's head. Reaching for [Counterspell](https://www.dndbeyond.com/spells/2619072-counterspell), the sorcerer tries to pinch it out as he pinched out the flameskull's fireball, and there's nothing there to pinch. It isn't a spell; it's her. Folding over in the corner, he laughs until the tears run and his head feels as though it's coming apart.

Bilwin can't resist. "That's what you get for disrespecting your elders."

"Exactly what he said. Ten points to the dwarf."

Watching his friends clutch their heads and laugh through the pain, Bilwin feels something come back to him from the years he can't reach, and he calls on the only friends who were there for it. Six ethereal dwarves rise around him as [spirit guardians](https://www.dndbeyond.com/spells/2619078-spirit-guardians), wheeling slowly through the little cottage, and the old woman doesn't care for what they do to her. Then Mond, still giggling, squirts a [spray of poison](https://www.dndbeyond.com/spells/2618875-poison-spray) into her face from the corner, and that she doesn't find funny at all. "Minus twenty!"

She starts to grow.

The skin of her face goes hard and copper-colored, and she keeps growing, past the table and past the door, until the companions are pressed flat against the walls. Then the walls go, and the roof with them, out into the garden in a burst of splintered timber and screaming wind chimes. Where Granny Verdigris stood there's a [copper dragon](https://www.dndbeyond.com/monsters/5194872-adult-copper-dragon), its scales gone green at the edges, wearing the old woman's expression.

"Holy crap!" Gven brings Tempest Edge around twice and both strikes land, the second deep enough that the dragon flinches, and the spectral dwarves close in and it flinches again.

Squaring up to it in the wreck of the garden, Shellbert tells it once more to get off his lawn, and the dragon rakes him three times. The first set of claws misses. The rage takes the worst of the other two, but nothing takes the acid hissing on his shell. The tortle takes stock. "Sizzling. Ow."

Backing away across the rubble, Dolor sends [three crackling beams of energy](https://www.dndbeyond.com/spells/2619161-eldritch-blast) into the dragon one after another, and all three hit.

That's enough for her.

Sitting back on her haunches amongst the wreckage of her own house, the dragon holds up a claw. "All right, all right. Fine. You can have your power crystal, it's over there. That was worth it, though. The turtle is very funny. The dwarf didn't score many points, but he was entertaining. I think I'm glad we didn't get the hurdy-gurdy. Until next time." Her wings open, and she's gone over the mountains before any of them can answer.

Digging through the rubble of the chair and fireplace, Dolor finds the crystal, a gemstone the size of a large marble, cut as his parents cut the stones in their shop. Light goes into it and comes back out. He closes his hand around it.

Before turning back into a halfling, the wolf trots over to what's left of the favorite rug and relieves himself on it.

Stomping off towards the skiff, Shellbert doesn't look back. "I've got something to say to that pilot."

Looking at the stone in Dolor's hand, Mond works it through out loud. "If we give him this, everyone on that colossus dies. If we don't, everyone dies anyway, because he's a sitting duck without it."

"Wibbly wobbly," says Landro. "Timey-wimey."

Grindlefoot likes his life the way it is, and when Mond points out that means everyone has to go through all of it again to get here, the halfling accepts that.

The skiff starts to lift the moment Shellbert and Dolor are aboard. Grindlefoot, Bilwin and Gven run for it and make it, and Mond opens a [gate](https://dnd5e.wikidot.com/spell:arcane-gate) onto the deck with the second piece of the rod and steps through as it climbs. The instant the sorcerer's boots touch the boards, it rises high and fast and turns east, and they spend the hour back to Landro catching their breath.

---

Shellbert is first down the shaft, shouting at anyone in his way to get out of it, and at the bottom he holds out a hand. "Give me that."

"No." Dolor keeps walking. "I'm giving it to the pilot."

The pilot is asleep in the chair again when they reach him. Dolor holds out the crystal with the tortle directly behind him, and Shellbert leans over his shoulder. "What the hell is the matter with you?"

Blinking awake, the pilot reaches, and stops. "Oh. Oh, wow. You actually did it."

Bilwin gets to the important question. "Where's our money?"

Shellbert hefts the axe to give the man the flat of it and someone shouts "Wait!"

Dolor, the crystal still in his other hand, punches the pilot square in the face instead and lays him out, back into his very important chair.

"Wait a minute! I'm just the pilot. The engineers said they needed it, I only drive the thing. Take it up with them, they have the money. I trust them with my life."

Folding her arms, Gven isn't having it. "Do you trust them with a dragon? You told us she was a little old lady."

"That's what I was told!"

Shaking his head, Shellbert sums it up. "That was a feisty old lady."

Dolor swishes his tail, "Old lady my ass."

Rubbing his jaw, the pilot tries to find the bright side. "Did she at least serve you tea?"

Thinking it over, Bilwin concedes the point. "In all fairness, she did make tea."

Mond suggests sending the pilot to the sleeping quarters down the shaft, where anyone who walks in falls asleep beside the woman on the bunk. Someone points out that the room probably isn't cursed yet, and it takes the others another minute to talk him out of it.

Standing in front of the platform, Dolor looks at the chair, at the man in it, and then at the stone in his own hand. "He gets it. Whatever happens after, they need it for the war."

He puts the crystal in the pilot's hand. The man flops it into the cradle on his armrest, and the room goes dark.

---

When they can see again they're standing in the conservatory, amongst the harp and the harpsichord and the row of chairs. The door is shut, there's no Shellbert, and the carpet where three bodies lay is empty.

"I was in there," says Landro, from the top of Mond's staff. "I am in here. I would like to think about that for a while."

Dolor looks at the shut door, at the empty carpet, and back at the door. "I say we barricade that and take a nap."
